The problem

Bid tracking eats most of a workweek.

For small and mid-market contractors, the bid cycle is one of the largest unmeasured costs in the business. Most of it doesn't need a human.

Today

How most teams handle it

Read blueprints by hand to pull out line items. Two to three hours per project.

Email subs one at a time. Copy, paste, customize, send. Repeat ten to twenty times.

Chase responses in spreadsheets that go out of date the moment they're updated.

Lose bids to follow-up gaps — not because the work was wrong, but because nobody flagged the deadline in time.

Build the schedule in Excel once the bids finally land — usually under deadline pressure.

8–10h per project manager, per week
With BuildFlow

What changes

Upload the PDF. Line items are extracted in seconds. You review the list before anything goes out.

Bid requests draft themselves. Sent in one batch after you confirm. Tone and scope tailored per sub.

One dashboard for the whole cycle. Received, pending, and late, updated as responses come in.

Deadline reminders that fire on their own — 48 hours out, 24 hours out, and when a bid is past due.

Schedule generated from confirmed bids, ready to export to Sheets, CSV, or PDF.

~1h to review, approve, and send
Features

Built for the bid cycle, not retrofitted from elsewhere.

Six modules that cover the work between blueprint and groundbreaking. Designed for shops running 5 to 50 projects a year.

Blueprint parser

Reads PDF blueprints and scope documents, then returns a structured list of line items — partitions, MEP, finishes — for review before anything is sent.

Replaces 2–3 hrs per project

Bid request drafting

Drafts each request against the right scope, with the project details a sub actually needs. You edit and approve from one screen, then send the batch.

Replaces 1–2 hrs per project

Bid tracker

One dashboard for every open bid: received, pending, and overdue. Follow-up emails go out on the cadence you set, without anyone remembering to send them.

Replaces 2–3 hrs per week

Deadline reminders

Automated alerts at 48 hours, 24 hours, and post-deadline — to the sub and to the PM. Routed by email and surfaced in the dashboard.

Reduces missed-deadline rate

Schedule generation

Once bids are confirmed, the tool maps scopes onto a phased schedule with dependencies and target dates. Export to Google Sheets, CSV, or PDF.

Replaces 3–5 hrs per project

Inbox routing

Connects to your existing inbox, files incoming bid responses against the right project, and surfaces messages that need a human reply.

Replaces ~1 hr per day

Who it's for

Built around three working patterns.

If any of these sound like your shop, BuildFlow is designed for the way you already work.

The owner-operator

Running the business, the bids, and the schedule from one inbox. The hours lost to chasing subcontractors are hours not spent on the next sale.

Typical fit: 5–15 employees, 2–6 active projects

The growing GC

Two or three PMs juggling overlapping bid cycles. Spreadsheets work until they don't — usually right before a deadline.

Typical fit: 15–40 employees, 6–15 active projects

The specialty contractor

Bidding into many GCs across electrical, mechanical, or finish work. Speed of response is often the deciding factor on award.

Typical fit: 10–30 employees, high bid volume
